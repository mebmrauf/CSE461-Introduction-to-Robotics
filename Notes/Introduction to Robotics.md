# Introduction

## Why Intelligent Machines?

Intelligent machines are **robots or smart computers that can think and work like humans** to some extent. We use them because they give us many benefits:

### Energy Saving

**Easy meaning:**
They use **less energy** than humans or old machines for the same work.

**Example:**
A **smart washing machine** adjusts water and electricity according to clothes, saving energy.

### Accuracy

**Easy meaning:**
They can do **work very correctly without mistakes.**

**Example:**
A **robot arm** in a car factory can **fix parts perfectly in the exact position every time**.

### Convenience

**Easy meaning:**
They make our **lives easier** by helping us with work.

**Example:**
Using **Google Maps** (an intelligent system) makes it easy to find the best route.

### Efficiency

**Easy meaning:**
They can **do work faster and better** using fewer resources.

**Example:**
A **robot can assemble phones quickly and perfectly,** producing many phones in less time.

### Adaptability in Dynamic Environment

**Easy meaning:**
They can **adjust to changes around them and keep working well.**

**Example:**
A **self-driving car** can adjust its speed and direction if there is traffic or if the weather changes.

### Perform Dull, Dirty, Difficult, and Dangerous Jobs

**Easy meaning:**
They can do work that is **boring, dirty, hard, or risky for humans**.

**Examples:**

- **Dull:** A robot sorting 10,000 screws daily (boring for humans).
- **Dirty:** Robots cleaning toxic waste areas.
- **Difficult:** Robots used in precise surgeries.
- **Dangerous:** Bomb disposal robots or robots working in deep mines.

## **Law of AI Robot**

1. A robot must not harm human being, nor through in action allow one to come to harm.
2. A robot must always obey human beings, unless that is in conflict with the first law.
3. A robot must protect from harm, unless that is in conflict with the first two laws.
4. A robot always should have a kill switch.

## **Thumb Rules on the decision of a Robot Uses**

- The first rule to consider, what is known as the Four D of Robotics, i.e. is the task dirty, dull, dangerous, or difficult? If so, a human will probably not be able to do the job efficiently. Therefore, the job is appropriate for automation or for robotic labor.
- The second rule is that a robot may not leave a human jobless. Robotics and automation must serve to make our lives more enjoyable, not miserable.
- A third rule involves asking whether you can find people who are willing to do the job. If not, the job is a candidate for automation and Robotics.
- A four rule of thumb is that the use of robots or automation must make short-term and long-term economic sense.

## **Uncrewed Vehicle**

**A vehicle that works without a human inside it.** It can be controlled from far away or work by itself.

### **Types of Uncrewed vehicle (UGV)**

- Underwater vehicle
- Surface water vehicle
- Ground vehicle
- Air vehicle
- Space vehicle

### Remote Control Vehicle (RC)

**Easy meaning:**
A **vehicle controlled by a person using a remote** from a distance.

**Example:**
RC cars or toy helicopters that you control using a remote.

### Unmanned Ground Vehicle (UGV)

**Easy meaning:**
A **robot vehicle that moves on the ground without a driver.**

**Example:**
A robot used by the army to find and remove bombs on the ground.

### Unmanned Aerial Vehicle (UAV)

**Easy meaning:**
A **flying vehicle (drone) without a pilot inside.**

**Example:**
Camera drones used for photography or surveillance.

### Unmanned Combat Aerial Vehicle (UCAV)

**Easy meaning:**
A **military drone used for attacks without a pilot inside.**

**Example:**
A drone that can carry missiles for airstrikes in defense missions.

### Miniature UAV (SUAV)

**Easy meaning:**
A **small-sized drone used for short missions.**

**Example:**
Small camera drones used by police for monitoring crowds.

### Delivery Drone

**Easy meaning:**
A **drone that delivers packages.**

**Example:**
Amazon’s drones delivering parcels to customers.

### Micro Air Vehicle (MAV)

**Easy meaning:**
A **very small drone, often the size of a bird or insect.**

**Example:**
Tiny drones used to spy or monitor indoor spaces.

### Target Drone

**Easy meaning:**
A **drone used as a target for training the military** to practice shooting.

**Example:**
Air forces use it to test their missile systems.

### Autonomous Spaceport Drone Ship

**Easy meaning:**
A **robotic ship in the ocean where rockets land automatically.**

**Example:**
SpaceX uses these ships to land reusable rockets on the sea.

### Unmanned Surface Vehicle (USV)

**Easy meaning:**
A **robot boat that moves on water without a human on it.**

**Example:**
A boat used to check water pollution in lakes.

### Unmanned Underwater Vehicle (UUV)

**Easy meaning:**
A **robot vehicle that moves underwater without a human inside.**

**Example:**
Used for exploring the ocean or checking underwater pipelines.

### Remotely Operated Underwater Vehicle (ROUV)

**Easy meaning:**
An **underwater robot controlled by a person using a cable and remote.**

**Example:**
Used for repairing underwater oil pipelines or studying sea life.

### Autonomous Underwater Vehicle (AUV)

**Easy meaning:**
An **underwater robot that works on its own without direct human control.**

**Example:**
Used for mapping the ocean floor automatically.

### Uncrewed Spacecraft (Robotic Spacecraft or Space Probe)

**Easy meaning:**
A **spacecraft that travels in space without astronauts, controlled from Earth or working automatically.**

**Example:**

- **Mars Rover (robot vehicle exploring Mars)**
- **Voyager probes (exploring outer space)**

## How Uncrewed Vehicles Are Useful

### **Reduce Risk to Human Life**

They can **work in dangerous places** so humans stay safe.

**Examples:**

- **Bomb disposal robots (UGV)** remove bombs safely.
- **ROUVs** check underwater pipelines so divers are not at risk.
- **UCAVs** perform military missions without risking pilot lives.

### **Explore Hard-to-Reach Areas**

They can go where humans **cannot easily go**.

**Examples:**

- **UAVs** can fly into storm areas for weather research.
- **Mars rovers (uncrewed spacecraft)** explore Mars for science.
- **AUVs** explore deep ocean floors.

### **Save Time and Work Continuously**

They can **work faster and for longer periods without breaks.**

**Examples:**

- **Delivery drones** can deliver packages quickly.
- **USVs** can monitor water bodies for days without stopping.

### **Reduce Costs**

They reduce **travel and labor costs.**

**Examples:**

- **Delivery drones** lower delivery expenses.
- **UAVs** can survey land or inspect power lines without needing helicopters.

### **Perform Repetitive or Boring Tasks**

They handle **tasks that are boring for humans.**

**Examples:**

- Agricultural drones spraying crops.
- RC robots sorting items in warehouses.

### **Collect Data Efficiently**

They can **gather data accurately for research and monitoring.**

**Examples:**

- UAVs taking aerial photos for mapping or disaster response.
- AUVs collecting data on ocean temperature and pollution.

### **Increase Safety in Space Missions**

**Uncrewed spacecraft** explore space without risking astronauts.

**Examples:**

- Satellites monitoring Earth’s weather and climate.
- Space probes studying other planets.

### Why is this important for us?

- We **stay safe while work gets done** in risky environments.
- We **save time and money** in delivery, research, and monitoring.
- We **get information for science and weather forecasting.**
- We **make work faster and easier**, reducing human effort.
- We **protect human lives in defense and rescue operations.**
